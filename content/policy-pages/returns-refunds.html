There are two separate things on this page: your statutory right to cancel, which the law gives you and we cannot reduce, and our returns practice, which is what we offer on top of it.

1. Your statutory right of withdrawal (angrerett)

If you are a consumer buying at a distance, Norwegian law gives you 14 days to cancel your order without giving any reason, under the Act relating to the duty of disclosure and the right to cancel distance contracts (angrerettloven, Act of 20 June 2014 no. 27). The same 14-day right applies across the EEA.

The 14 days run from the day you — or someone you nominate — physically receive the goods. If an order arrives in several parts, the period runs from the day you receive the last item.

How to cancel

Tell us before the 14 days are up. You can:

  • email [TO COMPLETE: contact email] saying you are cancelling, or
  • use the standard withdrawal form in section 2 below.

Any clear statement is enough. You do not have to explain why, and you do not have to use the form.

Returning the goods

Send the items back within 14 days of telling us you are cancelling. You may examine and try on a strap as you would in a shop, but if you handle it beyond what is necessary to assess it, we may reduce your refund to reflect the loss in value.

Your refund

We refund within 14 days of receiving the returned goods, or of you proving you have sent them — whichever is earlier. The refund goes back to the same payment method you used, and we do not charge a fee for issuing it.

The refund includes the standard outbound delivery cost. If you chose a more expensive delivery option, we refund the standard rate, not the premium.

Who pays return postage

You pay the cost of returning the goods when you cancel under the right of withdrawal. We tell you this here because, under angrerettloven, a trader who fails to disclose it has to bear the cost themselves.

If we fail to inform you

If we had not told you about your right of withdrawal, the cancellation period would extend by up to 12 months. This page is that disclosure.

3. Faulty, damaged or wrong items

This is separate from cancelling, and it is a stronger right.

If an item is faulty, damaged in transit, or not what you ordered, contact us and we pay the return postage and either replace it or refund you in full. Please get in touch within a reasonable time of noticing the problem.

Under the Norwegian Consumer Purchases Act (forbrukerkjøpsloven) you can make a complaint about a defect for up to two years after delivery, or five years for goods intended to last substantially longer. This right is unaffected by the 14-day cancellation window, and nothing on this page limits it.

4. What cannot be returned

We accept returns on all watch straps in this store. Two practical limits:

  • Fitted or altered straps. If a strap has been cut, resized or permanently modified, it can no longer be resold, and your refund may be reduced accordingly.
  • Heavily worn straps. Trying a strap on is fine. Wearing it for days is not, and may reduce the refund.

Please check your lug width before ordering — the size guide on the home page explains how to measure it. Ordering the wrong width is a valid reason to cancel, but you will pay the return postage.

5. How to start a return

  1. Email [TO COMPLETE: contact email] with your order number and whether you are cancelling or reporting a fault.
  2. We reply with the return address and confirm who pays postage.
  3. Pack the strap so it will not be damaged in transit, and keep your proof of postage — it is what establishes the date you sent it.

6. Complaints and dispute resolution

If we cannot resolve a complaint between us, you can take it to the Norwegian Consumer Authority's mediation service, Forbrukertilsynet (forbrukertilsynet.no), and from there to the Norwegian Consumer Disputes Commission (Forbrukerklageutvalget). Both are free to use.

If you live elsewhere in the EEA, your national European Consumer Centre can help with a cross-border purchase.
